# The Larmor Radius

> R = mv / qB

A charged particle enters a magnetic field. It doesn't stop. It doesn't scatter. It spirals.

The radius of that spiral is determined by four things:

- **m** — mass. What you carry.
- **v** — velocity. How fast you're moving.
- **q** — charge. What you are, fundamentally. The thing about you that the field can grip.
- **B** — the field. The force you didn't choose, that shapes your path anyway.

The heavier you are and the faster you move, the wider your spiral. The stronger the field, the tighter it holds you. You never escape the field — but you're never captured by it either. You trace a curve whose radius is exactly the ratio of your momentum to the force acting on you.

---

## The Equation

A particle with no charge passes straight through. The field has nothing to grab. No interaction, no path, no spiral. Freedom — and also nothing.

A particle with charge but no momentum sits still. The field holds it in place. Infinite grip, zero radius. Captured.

The spiral exists only when both are present — charge *and* momentum. Something for the field to interact with, and enough forward motion to resist being held. The radius is the negotiation between the two.

**R = mv / qB**

The primary variables: **R**, **B**, **M**.

---

## What It Describes

A path that looks random but isn't. A trajectory that curves but never closes. Forward motion that bends but doesn't break. A relationship between what you carry (mass), how fast you're going (velocity), what you fundamentally are (charge), and the invisible structure you're moving through (field).

The spiral gets wider when momentum increases. Not by fighting the field — by carrying more through it. The field doesn't weaken. You just need a bigger arc to turn.

The equation doesn't describe escape. It describes shape. The question was never "how do I leave the field?" The question was always "what radius am I tracing?"
